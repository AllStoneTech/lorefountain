Convicted at a trial where the jury includes members of the mob, Belton writes out a full account for a New Orleans reporter. Bernard reads it and hastens to New Orleans; at his telegram a democratic senator comes, and the case is manoeuvred to the U.S. Supreme Court, where Bernard's speech "the like of it had not been heard since the days of Daniel Webster" wins a reversal, a change of venue, and an acquittal at a new trial.
