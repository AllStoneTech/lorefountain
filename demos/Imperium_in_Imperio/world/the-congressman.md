The congressman from the [[Winchester]] district, a polished orator with a national reputation. Belton secretly visits him at night; he purges the oration of its impurities of speech, gives him access to his library, and later congratulates him on his "triumph over oratory" and laments his "defeat by prejudice." He declines to be a judge. His praise of the speech leads the Winchester correspondent of the *Richmond Daily Temps* to send it to the editor.
