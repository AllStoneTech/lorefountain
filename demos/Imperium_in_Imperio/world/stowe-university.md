A Nashville school named for Harriet Beecher Stowe and run by Northern philanthropy, "scarcely more than a normal school with a college department attached." It has five buildings: a men's dormitory, two for women, a recitation hall, and the teachers' mansion. It has about three hundred and sixty students, young women outnumbering young men two to one. It is where Belton sees a colored teacher seated with the faculty, learns that the teacher does not eat with them, and leads the students in the "Equality or Death" rebellion that makes the faculty give way.
