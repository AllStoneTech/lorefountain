# Imperium in Imperio

## Premise

[[Belton Piedmont]] and [[Bernard Belgrave]] are enrolled on the same day in the colored school at Winchester, Virginia, in October 1867, rivals under a teacher who hates one and loves the other. Years later, after separate roads of cruelty and loss, Belton brings Bernard to Waco, Texas, tests him with a mock execution, and reveals a hidden nation: a government with its own Congress, army, and treasury, organized to win the rights the United States refuses to protect. Bernard becomes its president. Belton is its conscience. When a lynching and an approaching war with Spain push [[The Imperium in Imperio]] toward revolt, only one of them can be right, and the Imperium's own law decides what happens to the one who will not follow.

## Setting

America from October 1867 through the eve of the Spanish–American War (the novel gives no year for the war chapters, and 1898 comes from the historical Lake City murder): a small Virginia town and its colored school in a condemned Baptist church; the dormitories of [[Stowe University]]; a senator's mansion in Washington; a Richmond post office; a Louisiana college town terrorized by a secret white society, the Nigger Rulers; a Norfolk law office; and, hidden in plain sight outside Waco, Texas, a campus called [[Jefferson College]] that is really a capitol.

## Key Characters

- **[[Belton Piedmont]]** — poor, dark-skinned, the better orator, who believes rights must be won inside the Union.
- **[[Bernard Belgrave]]** — wealthy, the mulatto son of a senator and Fairfax Belgrave, who becomes the Imperium's president and its most dangerous idea.
- **[[Antoinette Nermal]]** — Belton's wife, excluded from her church for adultery when their child is born white, and later vindicated.
- **[[Viola Martin]]** — Bernard's great love, whose choice shapes everything he does afterward.
- **[[Tiberius Gracchus Leonard]]** — the teacher who torments Belton, spoils Bernard, and later serves as Bernard's political fixer.
- **[[Berl Trout]]** — the Imperium's Secretary of State, who fires on Belton and then betrays the Imperium; the narrator of the novel's papers.

## Synopsis

Sequence 1 follows Belton and Bernard from their first day in Leonard's school to the cistern revenge at their graduation. Sequence 2 follows Belton from Editor King's check through Stowe University, the Equality or Death rebellion, and the socks. In 3, Bernard learns who his father is, goes to Norfolk, meets Viola, and wins a contested seat in Congress; in 4, Belton marries Antoinette, loses two jobs, goes through the nurse's disguise, and flees when their child is born white; in 5, he is thrown off a train, hanged by the Nigger Rulers, survives the dissecting table by killing Dr. Zackland, and is saved by Bernard before the Supreme Court. In 6, Viola refuses to marry Bernard and takes her own life, leaving a letter that turns his grief into a vow of racial separation. In 7, Belton brings Bernard to Waco, tests him with the mock execution, reveals the Imperium, and Bernard becomes its president; the war with Spain and the murder of Postmaster Cook follow. In 8, the Congress is called to war; Belton's speech turns the vote to a five-point resolution, Bernard secretly replaces it with a Plan of Action to seize Texas, and Belton, who cannot support it, resigns, which in the Imperium means death. Bernard commands the firing squad himself, and Berl Trout, one of the five who fired, betrays the Imperium to prevent a catastrophe.

## Themes

Citizenship without protection; integration against separation; loyalty to a country that withholds its own promises; the cost of leadership when the people you love stand on the other side of a principle; and Griggs's own argument that a people denied lawful redress will build a lawful society of their own.
