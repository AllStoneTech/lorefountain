# How do you end a tragedy on a confession?

The novel ends with Berl Trout's written statement, explaining why he betrayed the Imperium. In the screenplay it becomes voice-over. Open questions for the next draft:

- The novel never shows or says how Trout reveals the Imperium; the screenplay stays silent too. Should the film show it, or only hold on the grave?
- Does the film end on Belton's grave, on Bernard's vow at the grave, or on Trout's confession? The book lands on the last of these, but film audiences usually remember the last image, and Bernard's face at the grave is a stronger one.
- If Trout is executed offscreen, is that a line of title text or a silence?

Not decided. Left here as a real open question for whoever picks this up next.
