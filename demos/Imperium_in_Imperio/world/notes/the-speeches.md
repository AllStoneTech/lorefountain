# The speeches

The novel is built around long orations: the commencement contest, Bernard's Supreme Court plea, Bernard's message to the Congress, and Belton's answer. The current draft keeps the key passages of Bernard's message and Belton's answer in the novel's own wording, cutting between them (the Labor, Education and Courts sections are the most condensed), and keeps Bernard's Supreme Court speech off screen: the novel gives no text for it.

- Open question: let Belton's speech run uncut as one take, since it is the one that changes the plot.
- Intercut the audience's reaction, because the novel's best moments are the crowd turning.
