Mr. V. M. King, editor of the *Richmond Daily Temps*, born and reared in Virginia, educated at a Northern university and a sojourner in England; a Democrat in politics and an outspoken opponent of vote-cheating and lynching. He prints Belton's oration and reads it to his wife with the words "it has come at last"; he dreams of swine and oaks, and pays for Belton's college on one condition, that Belton recognize "a good side to the character of the worst class" of white people. He is present at Belton's Stowe commencement, and later gets him his Richmond teaching post.
