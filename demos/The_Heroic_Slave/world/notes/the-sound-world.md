# The sound world

The use case of this demo is a *historical, voice-and-ocean-driven* audio drama. The cues in the scripts follow a small design plan so a sound designer can build from the Cue Sheet.

## Recurring motifs

| Motif | Where | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Flint on file — a bright spark and scrape | Tinder-box (1x02); files in the hold (1x05) | Survival becoming liberation |
| Wind through pine, then through rigging | 1x01 → 1x06 | The same wind: the forest and the ocean are both "free" |
| Church bells, distant | 1x01, 1x02 | A religion that cannot console him; Madison "shuns the church" |
| Chains | 1x03 onward | Always in the mix until the wharf at Nassau, then silence |
| The Narrator's string note | Opening/closing each episode | A single sustained note, never a melody |

## Music

Sparse and acoustic: low strings, a solo voice (hymn fragment, wordless), a hand drum for the coffle's march and for the crowd at Nassau. No score during Madison's longest speeches; let the ambience carry them.

## Mix notes

- **Interiors are close and dry** (the Listwells' fireside); **exteriors are wide** (woods, wharf, deck).
- The tavern night (1x04) should be heard *through floorboards*: the bar-room is muffled and below, the chains come in later, from above and all around.
- The storm (1x06) is the loudest thing in the drama. Pull everything else out of the mix when it breaks.
