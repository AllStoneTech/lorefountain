# Language and casting

## Language

The source uses period terms and racial slurs in the mouths of Wilkes, Jack Williams, Tom Grant, the tavern company and the slave-keepers. This project keeps them, because the novella shows these men talking and the drama is an adaptation of a historical document. See the content note in `README.md`. The words belong to the speakers and are never used in narration, and Madison, Listwell and the narrator never say them.

Dialect: the tavern characters speak in the source's own eye-dialect ("alloys", "becase", "Virginy", "agin"). Cast them with a real regional ear rather than a caricature; the source's spelling is a guide, not a recording.

## Casting

- **Madison Washington** should be an actor who can be gentle and frightening in the same sentence. The text has him at his most eloquent when he is most controlled.
- **The Narrator** should not imitate Frederick Douglass, only his cadence.
- **Tom Grant and Jack Williams** carry the drama's ethical argument in a room with no Black voices in it; direct them so that Grant's reluctant fairness lands and Williams's contempt is allowed to curdle.
- Ensemble roles (the Nineteen, the coffle, the Nassau crowd) should be cast with Black actors; their sound is the drama's emotional payoff.
