# The Heroic Slave

> **Content note.** This work adapts an 1853 text and preserves its period language faithfully, including racial slurs spoken by slaveholders, slave-traders and sailors. They are kept because the source uses them to show how these men speak and think.

## Premise

In 1835 a Northern traveller in Virginia overhears a slave named [[Madison Washington]] talk himself into running for freedom. Six years later, the same man, [[Mr. Listwell]], sees him chained in a coffle bound for the New Orleans market. Two months after that, in a Richmond coffee-house, sailors argue about how nineteen "ignorant negroes" took the brig [[The Creole]] from her officers. Frederick Douglass's novella gives us only glimpses of its hero, "a guiding star on a stormy night"; this adaptation turns those glimpses into a sequence of voices in the dark.

## Setting

The Virginia pine woods in spring 1835; an Ohio farmhouse on a cold night in the winter of 1840; the road, tavern and wharves between Petersburg and Richmond in 1841; the brig [[The Creole]] on the Atlantic; and the harbour of [[Nassau]], where British law does not recognise men as merchandise. It is an audio world of wind, birds, chains, files on iron, ship's timber and, finally, a crowd cheering on a wharf.

## Key Characters

- **[[Madison Washington]]** — tall, strong, eloquent; a man who "loved liberty as well as did Patrick Henry".
- **[[Mr. Listwell]]** — the Ohio traveller who becomes an abolitionist because of what he overheard, and who tries, each time, to help.
- **[[Mrs. Listwell]]** — his wife; feeds the fugitive and fears for his cooking.
- **[[Susan]]** — Madison's wife, a house-servant who meets him in the pine woods for five years.
- **[[Tom Grant]]** — the first mate of the Creole, who tells the story of the mutiny and admits what he saw.
- **[[Jack Williams]]** — the braggart "old salt" who thinks negroes cowards and the mate a coward in turn.
- **[[Wilkes]]** — the tavern loafer who mistakes Listwell for a buyer of human beings.
- **[[The Narrator]]** — the authorial voice, a witness peering "into the dark" for a lost hero.

## Synopsis

Six episodes follow the four parts of the novella. **1x01** is the pine-wood soliloquy. **1x02** and **1x03** are one cold winter night at the Listwells' fireside, where Madison tells the story of his whipping, five years in the swamp, a forest fire, an old man's prayer, and the journey that ends with a letter from Windsor. **1x04** is the Richmond-road tavern, the slave gang in the bowling alley, Susan's death, and the three files slipped to Madison on the wharf. **1x05** is the Marine Coffee-house, where a boaster is made to listen, and **1x06** is the mutiny itself: the dying captain, the storm, the helm, and the wharf at Nassau.

## Themes

The inconsistency of calling the same trade piracy on the African coast and commerce on the Chesapeake; liberty as a principle ("the principles of 1776") applied to those it was meant to exclude; the courage that shows itself on the sea rather than on shore; and the witnesses (Listwell, Grant) who see the truth and are not all equally changed by it.
