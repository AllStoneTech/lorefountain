Part 4's opening indictment: the nation "execrate[s] and hang[s]" the wretch guilty of the African slave trade "on the coast of Guinea", yet "respect[s] and applaud[s]" the same traffic "on enlightened shores of the Chesapeake" — "the smooth and gliding phrase, AMERICAN SLAVE TRADE". The drama's moral spine; the narrator speaks it as 1x05's prologue. Real-world background, including the inter-state coastal trade and the 1808 end of imports, is context only (see `world/notes/the-real-creole.md`).
