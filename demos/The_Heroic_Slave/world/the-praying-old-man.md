One of the colored woodcutters; the only one who stays behind to pray. Madison comes down from his tree to him, gives him his last dollar for crackers and cheese, and watches from hiding as the old man is whipped thirty-nine lashes by fourteen armed men for having the money. Madison does not blame him: "the truthfulness of the old man's character compelled him to disclose the facts." The text does not say whether he is free or enslaved.
