# Winona

## Premise

[[Winona]] and [[Judah]] grow up free on an island in Lake Erie under [[White Eagle]], a white man who lives with the Seneca. When White Eagle is shot in the back, an English lawyer, [[Warren Maxwell]], happens to be there to find the body. Before he can take the children to England, slave traders put in a claim for them under the fugitive-slave act. Two years later Warren finds them on a Missouri plantation and the real chase begins.

## Setting

The Niagara frontier in the early 1850s; a Missouri plantation near Kansas City in 1856; a Missouri River steamer; the Underground Railroad's cabins in Kansas Territory; John Brown's camp in the hills near Lawrence; a Missouri cross-roads, court house and jail; the mountain above the Possawatomie; and back to the Grand Island Hotel on the Niagara frontier. Real events behind it: the fugitive-slave law, the Kansas-Nebraska fight, the sack of Osawatamie, and John Brown's Kansas war.

## Key Characters

- **[[Winona]]**: the island girl turned heiress; shy as a child, steady under bondage, the one who plans the jail rescue.
- **[[Judah]]**: her adopted brother, a Seneca-trained rider and shot whose oath against Thomson drives the middle of the story.
- **[[Warren Maxwell]]**: the young English lawyer who keeps a promise longer than he should have to.
- **[[Ebenezer Maybee]]**: innkeeper and conductor, the story's loud heart.
- **[[Bill Thomson]]** and **[[Colonel Titus]]**: the overseer and the planter, one cruel, one courteous, both English.
- **[[John Brown]]** and **[[Parson Steward]]**: the abolitionist captain and the preacher who keeps a rifle by his bed.

## Synopsis

**Issue 1 (Chapters I-II), *The Island*.** Winona and Judah meet two hunters on their island; a month later White Eagle is shot, and Warren Maxwell, an English lawyer sent to find a missing heir, is the one who says it is murder. **Issue 2 (III-V), *Magnolia Farm*.** The funeral and Warren's promise; slave traders claim the children; two years later Winona is Lillian's maid and Judah an overseer at Colonel Titus's plantation, where Judah tames a horse and is whipped for it. **Issue 3 (VI-VII), *The Crescent*.** Warren arrives at the farm, Judah tells him they will be sold, and on the steamer *Crescent* Warren lowers Winona into a boat in a thunderstorm. **Issue 4 (VIII-IX), *The Underground Railroad*.** They hide in Parson Steward's cabin, ride to John Brown's camp, and Warren, riding back, is shot and captured by Thomson's Rangers. **Issue 5 (X-XII), *The Cross-Roads*.** Warren is staked at a cross-roads until Colonel Titus stops the fire so he can be hanged lawfully; in the camp Judah asks Winona to marry him and is refused; Warren is tried and sentenced, and a boy called Allen Pinks nurses him. **Issue 6 (XIII-XIV), *Possawatomie*.** Allen Pinks is Winona; Brown, Maybee and Judah break Warren out of the jail, and on the peak above the Possawatomie Warren and Winona confess their love. **Issue 7 (XV-XVI), *The Ledge*.** Brown's hundred riders fight three hundred Rangers; Judah kills the Colonel and makes Thomson jump from a ledge; Winona finds him alive and stops Judah's second shot. **Issue 8 (XVI-XVII), *Jubilee*.** Thomson, dying, says who White Eagle was, who killed him and who killed Young Lord George; a tin box of papers at the Grand Island Hotel proves it, and the book closes in England and on a spiritual.

## Themes

Freedom as law, not as feeling: every rescue in the book fails or succeeds on a piece of paper. Violence and its limits: Steward's pistols, Brown's volley, Judah's oath, Winona's hand across the gun. Race and caste, argued out loud on a mountain. The novel's own epilogue is England and a knighthood; the comic keeps it but lets the reader decide what it costs.
