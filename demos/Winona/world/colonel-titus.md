An Englishman by birth who sank a small fortune into a Missouri plantation and horse-trading and became "one of the most bitter partisans on the side of slavery". His 1854 St. Joseph speech urged Missourians to vote in Kansas "at the point of the bowie-knife and revolver". He is courteous, hospitable and a believer in "decency and order and according to the process of law", the voice that takes Warren off the stake only to put him in front of a judge. He is, in the story's last reveal, the nephew who stands to inherit Carlingford and the man who killed White Eagle.
