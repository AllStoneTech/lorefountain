The young mulatto cook and head-waiter from a Missouri river steamer who, according to his own story, was stopped on suspicion at a ferry and jailed until sale. The jailers let him nurse Warren through the fever. He is Winona. Warren feels he knows the voice and the head-carriage; the jailer Owens only learns it when Thomson says "if it ain't Winona". This is the one place where the novel plays fair-play mystery with its own heroine.
