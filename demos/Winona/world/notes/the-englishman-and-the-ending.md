# The Englishman and the ending

The novel's title character spends most of the book being rescued, and then rescues someone herself: she goes into a Missouri jail disguised as a boy to nurse the man who is to be hanged. The book's ending, though, makes her a lady in England, makes the white hero her husband-to-be, and makes Judah, who has done most of the fighting, a knight "in the service of the Queen" who "never returned to America".

**Open questions.**

- Does the comic's last page keep the Queen's service and the knighthood, as the novel does, or end on the island and Aunt Vinnie's song? Current draft: both: the epilogue is told in captions on one page (Issue 8, page 18), followed by Aunt Vinnie's splash and a last page on the island. The knighthood is a fact; it is also the novel's way of closing the book on a Black hero without a place for him at home.
- Warren's rescue speech in Chapter XIII ("I understand the slavery question through and through") and Winona's "I cannot marry out of the class of my father" are the book's own thesis on caste. The scripts keep them but do not underline them.
- "John Brown ... was next heard of in the Virginia insurrection which ended so fatally": the comic gives this as a caption with no date.
