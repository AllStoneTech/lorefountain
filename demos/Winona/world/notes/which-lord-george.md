# Which Lord George?

The novel uses "Lord George" for two people.

- Chapter II, Mr. Pendleton: "young Lord George and his brother fell in love with the same woman"; "Lord George was found dead, shot in the back through the heart." A few lines later: "Lord George cannot live many months longer, he is eighty-five now."
- Chapter VI, Warren to the Colonel: "Your uncle died six months ago." He also says "Lord George's younger son, Henry".
- Chapter XVII: they will marry "with the sanction of her grandfather, old Lord George".

So the same title names the murdered young man, his father, and an old man who is dead in May and living a few chapters later. Thomson's confession in Chapter XVI says he was "Lord George's valet" who "shot Lord George in the back", so it is the *young* man there.

Decide whether to keep that, or to rename the old lord (e.g. "the old lord") in the lettering and leave the contradiction only in the source notes.
