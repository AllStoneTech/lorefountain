At Weston the town turns out with torches; the crowd votes to thrash, hang or burn him and drags him with a rope to the cross-roads sign-post. Thomson lights the fire at dawn. Colonel Titus rides in with constables and kicks the burning wood away, not out of mercy but because the prisoner is a British subject and must go through the courts: "let all things be done in decency and order and according to the process of law." Thomson rides off alone.
