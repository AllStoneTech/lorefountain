# Loomwake

## The pitch

Six weeks ago, every ship, station, and settlement in the Sable Verge picked up the same eleven-second burst on every channel at once — not a voice, not code anyone's cracked, just a *shape* of sound that made three separate species independently call it "a chord." Astronomers traced it to something that shouldn't exist: a structure the size of a small moon, dark and silent for as long as anyone's kept records, sitting where the old charts just say *hazard, do not approach*. It's called the Cael Loom now, after whoever built it. Before six weeks ago, most people weren't sure it was real.

Everyone with a reason to want it — and in the Verge, that's a lot of people — is now converging on the same coordinates. The player characters are aboard the *Loose Thread*, an independent salvage-and-transport ship that picked up the signal early and got there before the traffic did. That's the whole premise: you got there first, and now you're the only neutral party in a room that's about to get very crowded.

## Tone

Pulpy, fast, and a little bit haunted — competent crew, real stakes, banter that doesn't stop the plot from moving. Closer to a heist crew caught between bigger players than a grim survival story. The horror, where it shows up, is about identity and trust, not gore.

## The central question

The Cael built the Loom and then vanished so completely that not even their name survived anywhere but the Loom itself. The Vhel — a spacefaring, shapeshifting people scattered thin across a dozen systems for longer than anyone's written history — have their own oldest stories about a home they lost, and those stories have started sounding less like myth since the signal. Whether the Vhel are the Cael's descendants, their creations, or something that just moved into the wreckage after they left is the mystery the whole campaign orbits. The Loom itself is reactivating in stages, not all at once — which means the party, and the table, learn what it actually is piece by piece, usually in whatever order they're reckless enough to go looking.

## Who wants what

- **The Tessera Concern** — a salvage-and-extraction corporation that got a licensing claim filed within hours of the signal, through channels fast enough to make people ask who they'd bribed in advance. They want the Loom's technology, and they want it exclusive.
- **The Kindled Choir** — a militant faith that reads the signal as the start of a foretold Unveiling. They're not wrong that something's starting. They're dangerous specifically because their read on *what* isn't obviously wrong either.
- **The Verge Compact** — the loose mutual-defense pact of Verge settlements, scrambling a peacekeeping presence mostly to keep the Concern and the Choir from shooting at each other somewhere that might shoot back at everyone.
- **The Hollow Chain** — a smuggler-pirate cartel that doesn't care what the Loom is, only what it's worth before someone smarter shows up to lock it down.
- **The Vhel Diaspora** — not unified, not a faction the way the others are. Some Vhel think the Loom is a way home. Some think reactivating it is exactly what got their home taken in the first place, and want it left dark. One of them is already embedded deep inside another faction on this list, for reasons that are theirs alone until the table's earned them.

## Why this is unfinished, on purpose

This campaign has no ending written because it isn't supposed to have one yet — it's built to be picked up and continued at an actual table, not read to a conclusion. Several factions' arcs are still active, `world/notes/` holds real unresolved GM hooks rather than placeholder examples, and the last session in `scripts/` stops mid-scene. Whoever picks this up next is meant to decide what happens, not discover what already did.
