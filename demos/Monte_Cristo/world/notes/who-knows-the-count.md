# Who knows the Count's identity, and when the book tells us

The book reveals the Count's identity to the reader in chapter 25 (Dantès buys the Allées de Meilhan house as "Lord Wilmore"), long before most of the cast learns it. Chronological order and telling order differ:

- **Lord Wilmore, Sinbad, Busoni, Zaccone, Major Black.** One man, to the reader. In Paris the Count tells Andrea that Sinbad is Wilmore (ch. 56); in ch. 69 the narrator unmasks "Lord Wilmore" as the Count; Busoni is unmasked as the Count in ch. 82 ("the false abbé") and ch. 83.
- **Mercédès** knows him from the first meeting by his voice (ch. 89).
- **Albert** hears of his grievance from his mother (ch. 90), and by ch. 106 talks of "M. Dantès' house".
- **Fernand** hears the name in ch. 92 and shoots himself.
- **Maximilian, Julie, Emmanuel** are told in ch. 105. Valentine knows only her protector (chs. 100–101) until the signed letter in ch. 117, "Edmond Dantès, Count of Monte Cristo".
- **Villefort** learns it in ch. 111, **Danglars** in ch. 116.
- **Caderousse** hears the name whispered and the book does not print it (ch. 83).
- **Haydée** calls him "my lord" throughout; the book never has him name himself to her.
