The Roman banking house, in the Via dei Banchi near St Peter's (ch. 114), in whose name the "Englishman" buys Morrel's debts and extends them (chs. 28–30). Its Roman clerk is Peppino's informant; the Count's receipt for 5,100,000 francs from Danglars is payable there (chs. 104, 114). In 1838 it is the Count's banker in Rome, and Maximilian Morrel starts at the name (ch. 40).
