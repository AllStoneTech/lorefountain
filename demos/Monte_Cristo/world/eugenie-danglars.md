The banker's daughter, who does not wish to marry and flees to Brussels with her music teacher Louise d'Armilly, dressed as men, in chapter 97, on the night the contract is to be signed, travelling on a passport for "M. Léon d'Armilly, twenty years of age, artist" that the Count obtained for them. She is seventeen (chs. 54, 78), black-haired, with a dark mole at the corner of her mouth (ch. 53). She agrees to the Cavalcanti marriage only to keep her freedom and learns of her father's ruin from him (ch. 95). Andrea falls into her room at Compiègne and she tells him to kill himself (ch. 98); the next day they reach the Hôtel de Flandre in Brussels.
