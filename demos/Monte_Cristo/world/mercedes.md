A Catalan orphan of seventeen when the book opens, betrothed to Edmond and pursued by her cousin Fernand. After Edmond vanishes she marries Fernand eighteen months later in the church of the Accoules and becomes the Countess of Morcerf. In Paris she is the first to see through the Count; she begs for her son's life and, after Fernand's disgrace, leaves with Albert. Her father's surname, Herrera, is given only once, in chapter 91, when she offers it to Albert in place of the Morcerf name. Her portrait in Catalan dress hangs in [[Albert de Morcerf]]'s room. She recognises the Count by his voice and goes to him the night before the duel (ch. 89). After Fernand's death she and Albert give their fortune to the hospitals, she lodges in a rented room, and then returns to Marseille to the old Dantès house, where the Count finds her at thirty-nine looking fifty (ch. 112).
