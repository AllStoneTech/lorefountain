Mercédès's Catalan cousin, who posted Danglars's denunciation. Caderousse (ch. 27) traces his rise: drafted in 1815, he deserts after Ligny, wins rank in the Spanish war of 1823, becomes Count and, after serving in Greece, a lieutenant-general and peer. He tells the Danglars family in their opera box that he was inspector-general of Ali's troops (ch. 53). The attestation Beauchamp brings from Yanina says he surrendered the castle for two million crowns (ch. 84); Haydée says he sold her and her mother for 400,000 francs (ch. 86). The Count exposes his betrayal of Ali Pasha, Fernand learns the Count is Edmond Dantès (ch. 92), and he shoots himself after his wife and son leave (ch. 92). The Beauchamp article names him "Fernand Mondego"; both names are aliases so the two forms link. "Morcerf" is a shared alias with [[Albert de Morcerf]]; hover shows both.
