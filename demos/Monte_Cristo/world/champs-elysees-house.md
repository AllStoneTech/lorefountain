The Count's Paris residence, "No. 30, Avenue des Champs-Élysées" (chs. 47, 55): a house standing alone between a courtyard and a garden, with a pavilion reserved for the Count and separate apartments for Haydée behind a tapestried door. Bertuccio's gong code (ch. 46), Danglars's visit and the unlimited credit (ch. 46), Albert's request that the Count be his second (ch. 78), Mercédès's night visit (ch. 89) and Fernand's last visit (ch. 92) all happen here. Caderousse breaks in through the dressing-room window and is stabbed outside the garden wall (chs. 82–83). In his farewell letter the Count gives it, with the grotto and the château at Tréport, to Maximilian (ch. 117).
