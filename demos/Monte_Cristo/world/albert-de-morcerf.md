Viscount and only son of the Count and Countess of Morcerf, touring Italy in 1838. Carried off by Luigi Vampa during the Roman Carnival and freed through the Count, he invites him to Paris for 21 May. He is about to marry Eugénie Danglars, whom he does not want (ch. 54). After his father's disgrace he challenges the Count at the Opera (ch. 88); Mercédès's night visit to the Count stops the duel, and on the field he publicly apologises (ch. 90). He gives up his name and his possessions, takes Mercédès to a rented room, sells himself as a Spahi substitute for 2,000 francs, and sails for Algiers as Mercédès waves from the quay (chs. 91, 106, 112). Albert is "not yet twenty-one" in ch. 54, twenty-one in ch. 78, "scarcely twenty-two" in ch. 91. His father's income is about 50,000 francs a year (ch. 54).
