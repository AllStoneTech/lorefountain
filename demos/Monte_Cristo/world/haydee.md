The "beautiful Greek" seen at the Teatro Argentina, then in the Count's house in Paris. She is the daughter of Ali Tepelini, pasha of Yanina, and [[Vasiliki]], nineteen or twenty in 1838 (ch. 49) and four years old at her father's death (chs. 77, 86). Sold with her mother by Fernand to the slave-merchant El-Kobbir, she was educated by an Armenian, sold at thirteen to the Sultan (ch. 77), and bought from him by the Count with an emerald; El-Kobbir's receipt, read in the Chamber of Peers, calls her "eleven years of age" and seven years after her sale (ch. 86). She speaks Romaic and Italian (ch. 77), has the attendant [[Myrtho]], and tells the Count she loves him "as a father, brother, husband" (ch. 117). First seen in the Teatro Argentina in Rome; named first in ch. 45. In chapter 86 she tells the Chamber of Peers how Fernand betrayed her father, and in the last chapter she leaves with the Count.
