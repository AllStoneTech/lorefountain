# The Count of Monte Cristo

## Premise

[[Edmond Dantès]] is a mate of the *Pharaon*, nineteen and about to marry [[Mercédès]]. [[Danglars]] writes a false denunciation, [[Fernand Mondego]] posts it and [[Caderousse]] watches in silence, and [[Gérard de Villefort]], reading the address on the letter in Dantès's pocket, buries the case to protect his own father [[Noirtier]].

## Setting

Marseille in 1815, the Château d'If and the Mediterranean islands through 1829, then Rome during the Carnival of 1838 and Paris from May of that year, under Louis-Philippe.

## Key Characters

- **[[Edmond Dantès]]** — prisoner, then Sinbad, the Abbé Busoni, Lord Wilmore and the Count of Monte Cristo.
- **[[Abbé Faria]]** — the prisoner who educates him and leaves the treasure.
- **[[Danglars]], [[Fernand Mondego]], [[Gérard de Villefort]]** — the three betrayers.
- **[[Pierre Morrel]]** and **[[Maximilian Morrel]]** — the honest house he repays.
- **[[Haydée]]** — the Greek ward whose testimony ends the Count of Morcerf.
- **[[Albert de Morcerf]]**, **[[Valentine de Villefort]]**, **[[Benedetto]]** — the next generation caught in the fallout.

## Synopsis

Volume 1 (chapters 1–27) takes Dantès from the arrest and the dungeon to the treasure and the Pont du Gard inn. Volume 2 (28–47) saves the Morrels, then moves to Rome and Paris. Volumes 3 and 4 (48–95) build the vengeances: Danglars's ruin, Villefort's house of poisoners, the Cavalcanti impostors and, by chapter 92, Fernand's suicide; Caderousse dies at the Count's Champs-Élysées house in chapter 83. Volume 5 (96–117) finishes Danglars, Villefort and Benedetto, and ends on Monte Cristo island on 5 October, where Valentine is restored to Maximilian and the Count sails away with Haydée.

## Themes

Justice and private vengeance; Providence against the avenger's own will; money as power; the cost of a plan carried out too perfectly.
