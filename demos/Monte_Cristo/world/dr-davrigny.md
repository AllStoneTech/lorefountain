Villefort's physician and friend, who identifies Barrois's death as poisoning in chapter 79 and insists the crimes in the house must be faced. He first sees poison in Madame de Saint-Méran's death (ch. 73), tests the lemonade with syrup of violets (ch. 79), accuses Valentine and then withdraws (ch. 80), and keeps Noirtier on brucine for his paralysis. He has a daughter Madeleine and a niece Antoinette (ch. 72). He saw Valentine born (ch. 72) and pronounces her dead (ch. 102).
