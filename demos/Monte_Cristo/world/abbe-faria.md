Prisoner No. 27, reputed mad because he offers the governor millions for his liberty. He tunnels into Dantès' cell, teaches him languages, science and history, and deduces who wrote the denunciation. A cataleptic fit paralyses him, and he dies after a third attack, leaving Dantès the treasure. He wrote a treatise on a united Italy on strips of linen; in 1838 the Count recovers it from a jailer at the Château d'If, its epigraph "Thou shalt tear out the dragons' teeth", and prays at his cell, No. 27 (ch. 113). **Continuity:** chapters 14 and 16 date his arrest to 1811, while chapter 18 places it "a month" after Christmas 1807; chapter 14 gives twenty years as Spada's secretary, chapter 18 ten.
