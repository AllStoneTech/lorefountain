A Girondin under Robespierre, then Count and senator under the empire, vice-president of the Bonapartist club in the Rue Saint-Jacques (ch. 12). He is the addressee of the Elba letter. In 1838 he is a paralytic who can only blink and move his eyes; in chapter 75 he reveals to Franz that he killed Franz's father. Barrois is his servant; Valentine is the only person who reads him easily. He signals yes by closing his eyes, no by blinking, and raises his eyes for a wish; the right eye alone means Valentine, the left Barrois (ch. 58). He has been paralysed six years (ch. 58). He makes a will for the poor when Valentine is to marry Franz (ch. 59), tears it up and makes another leaving everything to Valentine (ch. 78), keeps the Bonapartist club statement in his secretaire (ch. 75, [[the club statement]]), and is the unnamed club president who killed [[General de Quesnel]] in a duel on 5 February 1815. The Count gives him a note that Ali takes to him before leaving Paris (ch. 112). **Continuity:** d'Avrigny says he has dosed Noirtier with brucine for three months (ch. 73), twelve months (ch. 80), and the Count says three years (ch. 100).
