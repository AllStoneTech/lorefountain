The poor house under the limes where old Dantès lived in the garret and where Edmond buried 150 louis for Mercédès under a fig-tree planted the day he was born. "Lord Wilmore" buys it (ch. 25). In 1838 the Count digs up the box and sends the money, through Bertuccio's letter, to Albert and Mercédès (ch. 91); Albert says 3,000 francs lie buried there (ch. 106). Mercédès ends her days in it, the Count having placed the whole house at her disposal (ch. 112).
