A stern royalist at Villefort's betrothal (ch. 6) and Valentine's grandmother in 1838. The Marquis dies of an apoplectic stroke in his carriage six leagues out of Marseille on the way to Paris and is reported in chapters 71–72; the Marquise, sixty-six and married forty years, reaches Paris with his corpse, tells of a white figure that came to her glass in the night (ch. 72) and dies in three attacks on the evening the contract is to be signed (ch. 73). D'Avrigny believes both were poisoned by brucine or strychnine (chs. 73, 79–80). The Marquis owned the Auteuil house (ch. 43). The name "Marquis de Saint-Méran" is stored here as an alias so one card serves both.
