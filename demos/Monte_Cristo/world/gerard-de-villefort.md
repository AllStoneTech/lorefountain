Deputy procureur at Marseille in 1815, twenty-seven and engaged to Renée de Saint-Méran. Dantès' letter is addressed to his own father, Noirtier, so he burns it and has Dantès shut up without trial; the king rewards him for the warning about Napoleon. By 1838 he is the king's procureur in Paris, sits on Caderousse's murder case, and is publicly denounced at Benedetto's trial as the father who buried him alive; he goes mad in chapter 111, digging for the child in his garden. Between: king's attorney at Nîmes and then Versailles, he is the man Bertuccio stabs in 1817 (chs. 43–44, 67); in 1838 he says he has been king's attorney twenty-five years (chs. 73, 103). He refuses an inquest on the Saint-Méran deaths (ch. 73), is told by d'Avrigny that Valentine is the poisoner (ch. 80), vows revenge for her death (ch. 103) and, believing his wife is the poisoner, tells her to kill herself before he returns from court (ch. 108).
