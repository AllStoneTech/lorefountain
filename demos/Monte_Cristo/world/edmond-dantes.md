The mate of the *Pharaon*, denounced on his betrothal day and imprisoned without trial in the Château d'If. There the Abbé Faria teaches him everything and leaves him the Spada treasure on Monte Cristo island. He escapes in 1829, repays the Morrels, and returns to Paris in 1838 as the Count of Monte Cristo to ruin [[Danglars]], [[Fernand Mondego]] and [[Gérard de Villefort]]. He is Sinbad the Sailor, the Abbé Busoni and Lord Wilmore as the plan requires, and each name is an alias here so it links to this card. The book's own dates for his arrest disagree; see [[the arrest of Edmond Dantès]].

**Who knows his name, and when.** The reader learns in chapter 25 that "Lord Wilmore" is Dantès. In Paris the Count tells Andrea that Sinbad is Wilmore (ch. 56) and plays Busoni and Wilmore as separate men to the police (ch. 69), where "Zaccone" is the Maltese shipowner's son he invents. He lets Caderousse die after whispering his name (the book does not print it, ch. 83); Mercédès knew him by his voice and says "Edmond" first (ch. 89); Albert learns the grievance from his mother (ch. 90) and speaks of "M. Dantès' house" by ch. 106; Fernand cries "Edmond Dantès!" (ch. 92); Maximilian, Julie and Emmanuel learn it in ch. 105; Villefort in ch. 111 and Danglars in ch. 116. Valentine is told only that he is her protector; Haydée calls him "my lord". The Count's own tally is "fourteen years of despair and ten of hope" (ch. 90). Paris takes him for a Maltese shipowner's son, an adventurer, a vampire or Cagliostro.
