A Corsican, "a soldier, a smuggler, in fact everything", who looks exactly like the smuggler who bandaged Franz's eyes. He is the Count's steward in Rome and Paris, and turns white at the word Auteuil: it is the house where he stabbed Villefort and dug up a living baby. His confession occupies chapters 43–45 (the "vendetta"). Named Giovanni Bertuccio, of Rogliano on Cap Corse, he swore a vendetta on Villefort after Villefort refused justice for his soldier brother, murdered at Nîmes in 1815. He stabbed Villefort in the Auteuil garden in September (1817, by the other chapters' dating), found the buried baby alive and carried it to the Paris foundling hospital, and his sister-in-law [[Assunta]] later reclaimed and named him Benedetto. Hidden in the shed at the Pont du Gard inn on 3 June 1829, he was arrested for the murder of the jeweller [[Joannes]] and freed in September when the Abbé Busoni confirmed his story, then sent to the Count. The Count's gong code is one stroke for Ali, two for Baptistin and three for Bertuccio (ch. 46). The Count tells Albert he holds the right of life and death over him (ch. 85). He visits Benedetto in La Force (ch. 107), carries the Count's letter to Albert (ch. 91), and at the end stays behind with Noirtier (ch. 111).
