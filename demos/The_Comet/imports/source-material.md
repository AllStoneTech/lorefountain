# Source material

> **CONTENT NOTE.** This work preserves the period language of a 1920 historical text faithfully, including a racial slur spoken by Fred and by the crowd on the roof in the story's last scene, and the period terms "Negro" and "colored." Nothing has been censored or softened, because the language is part of what the text depicts and what the adaptation answers to.

**Title:** "The Comet," chapter X of *Darkwater: Voices from within the Veil*
**Author:** W.E.B. Du Bois (1868–1963)
**First publication:** 1920 (*Darkwater*, Harcourt, Brace and Howe)
**Public domain status:** Published 1920; the author died in 1963. **Public domain in the United States only.** In countries with a life-plus-seventy-years rule it remains in copyright until 2034. Anyone using this demo outside the US should check local law.
**Full text:** [Project Gutenberg #15210](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/15210)
**Local copy:** `darkwater.md`, in this folder, with its Project Gutenberg license header intact (in the last section). This is the whole book (many essays and poems); only "The Comet" (chapter X, with its closing "A Hymn to the Peoples") was used. It is the section headed "X. The Comet" in the file.

## Provenance of this demo

Every entity, timeline event, and script was written against the full text of the story, read start to finish. The story is about 6,300 words long, in one continuous narrative with no chapter breaks; the audio drama divides it into six episodes at natural beats of place and sound.

## What this adaptation took from the story

All events, in order. All named places and streets (Broadway, Wall Street, Madison Square, Fifth Avenue, 42nd, 57th, 72nd, 110th and 135th Streets, Park Avenue, the Plaza, Spuyten Duyvel, the Williamsburg Bridge, Brooklyn, the Battery, Morningside Heights, the Metropolitan Tower). All the dialogue of the characters, kept in the source's own words wherever they have lines (including the note from J.B.H., the newspaper headline, the call-and-response "The rich and the poor are met together / The Lord is the Maker of them all," and every line of the last scene). Most of the narrator's lines are Du Bois's own sentences. The Hymn is spoken in full, with no lines cut or added.

## What this adaptation changed

- **A narrator is added** (`The Narrator`, `canon_status: tentative`). Du Bois's prose is third-person and interior; an audio drama needs a voice for what sound cannot show. Narration is almost entirely the text's own sentences, lightly shortened.
- **Interior thought becomes `(V.O.)`.** Where the text reports Jim's or Julia's thought ("Yesterday, he thought with bitterness, she would scarcely have looked at him twice") it is given to the character as voice-over.
- **Sound design is invented.** All `SFX:`, `MUSIC:` and `AMB:` cues are the production's, not Du Bois's, except where the text states a sound ("Boom!", "Honk! Honk!", "Clang--crash--clang!", the rocket "shrieked", the lapping waters, the rats and cobwebs).
- **A few connective lines are invented** and flagged here so they can be cut: the vault clerk's "Mind the damp!" (1x01); the walkers' overheard "Did you see it last night?" (1x01); a recorded newsboy cry (1x02); a one-line thought of the chest of gold (1x02, see `world/notes/who-is-the-chest-for.md`); Julia's "Sorry. Sorry." as she drives among the dead (1x03); Julia's "Thank you" on the roof (1x05); Jim picking his coat back up from the stone after the water (1x04); the rooftop `Julia (V.O.)` "The elf-queen. She would come soon." (1x05); the narrated "hundred places set" in the hostelry (1x02). No fact is added by any of them.
- **Episode breaks** fall at moments of sound, not at breaks in the source.
- **Fred's and Julia's relationship is left as the text leaves it:** "my darling," not "fiancé."

## Tentative entities (adaptation choices or inferences, not stated facts)

- **The Narrator** — an adaptation device.
- **Jim's Wife** and **Jim's Baby** — the text says only "a woman... brown, small, and toil-worn" with "the corpse of a dark baby," and that Jim carries "a baby's filmy cap." That she is his wife and the baby his child is the unmistakable implication, not a statement.
- **The Return of Sound** — a sound-design arc that exists only in the audio drama.
- **The timeline event "Jim and his wife"** — tentative for the same reason as the wife and baby.

## Known gaps

- Many background figures (the walkers, the watchman and guards, the businessman in the doorway, the woman at the signpost, the girls at the window and on the step, the dead of Harlem, the elevator boy, the stenographer, the clerk in the tower) have no entity files of their own; they appear only as sound and narration.
- The text gives no year, no day, no season, no name for the bank, Julia's surname, Fred's surname, J.B.H.'s name, or Jim's wife's name; this project does not invent them.
- The text never names the cause of death beyond a newspaper's "deadly gases expected." This project does not either (see `world/notes/what-the-comet-did.md`). The glossary note on the 1910 Halley's Comet scare is real history from outside the text, labelled as such.
- A slur and period terms stay as written (see the content note above); `world/notes/how-to-deliver-the-slur.md` lists the open production questions about delivery, such as an advisory.

## Review against the source

Every script, entity, timeline, glossary and notes file, `world/OVERVIEW.md` and the README were checked line by line against "The Comet" in `darkwater.md` (the story is the section "X. The Comet"; no other part of *Darkwater* is drawn on). The Hymn in 1x06 was compared word by word with the source by script: identical, nothing cut or added. Period language is kept as written: the slur (Fred and the crowd, 1x06) and "Negro" / "colored" are intact, and the content note is present in this file, `README.md`, `world/OVERVIEW.md` and 1x06.

**Confirmed against the text:** the sequence of events and the ending (only Jim and Julia found alive; her father and Fred arrive; Jim is tipped and asked his name; his wife arrives with the dead baby); the New York geography (Broadway, Wall Street, Madison Square, Fifth Avenue, the Plaza, 42nd, 57th, 72nd, 110th and 135th Streets, Park Avenue, Spuyten Duyvel, the Williamsburg Bridge, Brooklyn, the Battery, Morningside Heights, the Metropolitan Tower); and the quoted dialogue, the note, the headline and the call-and-response.

**Fixed (about 30 changes):**
- *Invented line in the source's mouth:* 1x06 gave THE CROWD an extra spoken "Where? There! Who -? Two!" before Fred speaks; the source has only an unworded "murmur and babel." Replaced by a stage direction. ("Two!" remains where the source has it.)
- *Chronology:* the timeline dated the boom "Noon" and Jim's waking "Afternoon," but the text has the comet already passed "this morning" and the street silent at "high-noon." Both events re-dated and the cause of confusion noted. The Wall Street entry no longer says "the clock says high noon" (no clock in the text).
- *Julia:* the dark room held photographs she took "last night" (not photographed "from her window"); she was "perhaps twenty-five"; she did not "wake" and her "first living voice" was not her own (removed); first appearance clarified.
- *The rockets and the honk:* the rocket from "almost at their feet" is not the "answering" rocket; Jim's is. Fixed in `the-rockets` and `the-honk`.
- *Crowd order:* "Here he comes" follows the lynching and "Shut up" lines; fixed in `the-crowd-at-the-tower`.
- *Unsupported claims softened or removed:* "two basements" (the bank); the cable-office entry said Jim "never sends" the call for help (the text does not say); "crossing" to the east side; the stench "meaning decay"; Julia's father "is why" he and Fred live; "tells them he is not in New York"; Jim "slept through" the warning; Jim "wakes" at the fountain; the exchange entry stated Jim "clears the room" (now flagged as implied); the Overview said the two were "the only two people left alive" (now "the only two either can find"); the house is not said to front on Fifth Avenue (flagged as an inference); the narrator entry understated how much of the prose the narrator speaks; gloved hand, "not eaten in a day," and a production note about a "table-read draft" in the scripts removed.
- *Leftovers:* `world/timeline/README.md` described another project's events (a "Mira," a "baby swap," episode 1x21); rewritten. `imports/README.md` called the story a novel.
- *References:* the only `.txt` mention left is the conversion note above, which says the `.txt` was removed; all references use `darkwater.md`.

**Unverifiable (the text is silent; left open or flagged):** who speaks "Long live the----" (the script gives it to Julia; the text does not say); the exact hour of the comet's passage; the cause of death; the year; where Julia took her photographs; why the vault clerk died where he did.

### Invented spoken lines (complete list)

Beyond the connective lines already listed under "What this adaptation changed," a full pass found these further lines that are not in Du Bois. None adds a fact. All can be cut.

- **1x01:** the vault clerk's "Mind the damp!"; Walker Three's "Did you see it last night?"; Jim's "Rat."; Jim's "Well."; Jim's "Oh, God."
- **1x02:** the recorded newsboy cry "Extra! Extra! Comet's tail sweeps past at noon!" (V.O.); Jim's "Oh --," "Guard! Guard!," "Watchman?," "Hello! Hello! Anyone!," "Cold. Still.," "No. No.," "Ah! Ah! Ah!," "Think it through.," "Seek the cellar.," "Hello? Hello?," "God."; Jim's V.O. thought of the gold ("And the gold. The chest. The gold, in the dark, for a hundred years, and no one to ever take it.").
- **1x03:** Julia's "You -"; Jim's "Hello! Open the door! Hello!"; Julia's "Sorry. Sorry."; Julia's "Hello! Hello!" at the river.
- **1x04:** Julia's "Hello?" and "Hello! Hello! Is anyone there? Hello!" (the text says only that she "called and called"); Julia's "Alone."
- **1x05:** Julia's "Thank you."; Julia's V.O. "The elf-queen. She would come soon."; Julia starting "Long live the -" alone (in the text the two cry "The world is dead" almost with one voice, and the next line is unattributed).
- **1x06:** none beyond the crowd's source lines (the invented crowd line was removed in this review).

Many other spoken lines are narration or reported thought in the source turned into speech or `(V.O.)` ("Here at last was peace," "The dull sheen of gold," "I have just room to squeeze through," "There was gas," "It was awful," and similar); these keep the source's words and are covered by the "interior thought becomes V.O." rule above.

Non-verbal business the production adds and which the text does not state: the money falling from Jim's hand and scattering when his wife calls his name (1x06); a tin plate and spoon for the roof meal (1x05); a stopped street car with its door open (1x02). None contradicts the text.

## Format conversion

The Gutenberg plain-text file (`darkwater.txt`) was converted to Markdown and the `.txt` removed (the project keeps source texts as well-formatted `.md`). Paragraphs were reflowed to one line each, headings and a linked table of contents added, verse, letters and quotations kept as line-preserved block quotes, and `_italic_` markers turned into Markdown emphasis. The Project Gutenberg header, footer and full license are kept verbatim in the "License and provenance" section at the end of the `.md`.

**Verification.** A script compared the word sequence of the `.md` body with the original text between the Gutenberg START/END markers (whitespace and markup characters normalized; added heading text excluded; headings compared case-insensitively). Result: **67,074 words in the body, identical in order and spelling**; 136 words of title page and the Gutenberg contents table were replaced by the generated contents list. The license section was compared character for character with the original header and footer (identical). Nothing was corrected, modernized or respelled.
