The county seat nearest the vineyard, named only as a disguise: the narrator says he calls it Patesville because that is not its name. Red brick market house with a four-faced clock and a nine o'clock curfew bell, a court-house, a jail, a few hotels, and four or five thousand people "of all shades of complexion." The slave trader's pen in [[Jeems McLean]]'s tale and the sale of [[Henry]] both run through it.
