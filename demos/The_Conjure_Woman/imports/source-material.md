# Source material

**CONTENT NOTE:** This adaptation preserves period language, including racial slurs and heavy dialect, because it adapts a historical text faithfully. It depicts slavery and its violence.

**Title:** *The Conjure Woman*
**Author:** Charles W. Chesnutt (1858–1932)
**First publication:** 1899, Houghton, Mifflin & Co.
**Public domain:** published 1899; author died 1932.
**Full text:** [Project Gutenberg #11666](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/11666)
**Local copy:** `the-conjure-woman.md`, with its Project Gutenberg license header (last section), in this folder.

## Provenance

Every entity, timeline event, and script was written from the full text of the seven tales and their frame passages.

## What this adaptation took

All seven tales' plots, names, and key lines of dialogue (in Julius's dialect), and each frame's twist. Original titles are kept.

## What it changed

- **Julius as host.** He addresses the camera to open and close each episode, with a recurring tag line ("I doan ax you ter b'lieve it. I des ax you ter set a minute.") and a one-line preview of the next episode; these are inventions.
- **The narrator's voice is dramatized.** John's narration becomes scenes.
- **Compression.** Tales are condensed to teleplay length; some details (minor named hands, the neighbors' names) are dropped.
- **Added scene beats** for the screen, such as the graveyard teaser in 1x05 and the close-up on the rabbit's foot.
- **Language kept.** Slurs and dialect appear in dialogue as in the source; entity and timeline files use neutral summary language.

## Invented by the adaptation (not in the book)

Beyond the host segments above, these are screen inventions, not source facts:

- **The cold open and tag of every episode** (Julius to camera, the preview of the next tale), and the "THE CONJURE WOMAN" title card.
- **Time cards:** "Six months later" (1x01; the book gives no interval), "Two weeks later" (1x02; the book says a week or two plus several days), "A month later" and "Two months later" (1x04; the book says the horse failed within a month and died after two or three months). The others ("Ten days later" in 1x07, "Several weeks later" in 1x05, "The end of their second year" in 1x05) follow the book.
- **Looks and manner not in the text:** Aun' Peggy as a tall woman in a head-handkerchief; the physical descriptions of Tenie, Becky, Solomon, Sandy, Primus, Jeems McLean, Jim McGee, the Guinea conjurer, Jube and Old Miss; the stranger in 1x03 in a torn white shirt. (The woman Solomon loves is left unnamed, as in the book.)
- **Added business:** the new vineyard sign and shipping mark in 1x01; Henry dancing with a girl; Sandy carrying a trunk; the boy who covers Tenie with his shawl; a congregation singing in the schoolhouse; Becky carrying Mose on her back when the trader chooses her; Mose buying his mother's papers in a clerk's office; Colonel Pendleton crumpling and keeping the horse trader's letter; Jube carrying his son away in his arms; the wolf and Mahaly's ghost together on the grave; Julius with a covered pail of honey; Jeff on the steamboat gangplank; the doll left on the sill.
- **Scenes the book gives only as report, played as direct speech:** Marrabo's regret at the swap and his order that Sandy go to Robeson, Dunkin's request for Tenie, Libbie's reason for refusing Jeems, Johnson sending the stray man back, Jim McGee's talk of the runaway, Dolphus finding the mule drunk, Becky's cry that she has been conjured, Mars Dugal's errand for the monkey wrench, and the speculator's letter about Jeff. Their wording is adapted from Julius's report.

## Review against the source

Every script, entity, glossary term, timeline event and note was read against the full text in `the-conjure-woman.md` (all seven tales and their frame passages) and corrected where the text disagreed or said nothing. The book's dialect and its slurs (nigger, pickaninny, "po' buckrah", and the rest) are kept as printed in every spoken line and in the quoted titles; nothing was softened. Two spoken lines that put dialect in the mouths of white characters the book gives no speech for (the foreman in 1x02, the man cleaning the spring in 1x03) were changed to plain English. Both README.md and this file carry the content note, and both refer to the source as `the-conjure-woman.md`.

**Fixed in scripts (about 30):**

- 1x01: the meeting was set "the next afternoon" (it is the same day as the visit); the mansion "burned" (the book says only that it fell victim to the war); Henry sold "on a block" and a calendar "eleven weeks" from spring (not in the book); Mars Dugal' as a "captain" (the book says he raised a company).
- 1x02: Marrabo described as "fleshy"; the foreman given dialect.
- 1x03: Jeems "thirtyish"; dialect for the man at the spring; the stranger's food "out of reach" (the book says he was given none); Solomon fetching his sweetheart home from Robeson County himself, by pass and note from Jeems, rather than her arriving by cart, and no guests from the next plantation.
- 1x04: Julius shown in the new suit before he had bought it; the opening line about Primus "telling all the tales himself" (the book says he calls Julius an old fool and has not spoken to him since); Pete "bareback" (the book has a bridle); the horse "breaking wind" (it is broken-winded).
- 1x05: Mose "a year old" and Nancy "gray" with "five babies"; the Colonel's wife where the book has Nancy's "ole missis"; Aun' Peggy's "bes' one I could git" and Becky's "I knows" (Becky is told by Nancy); the pipe on the older Becky; Becky "hat in hand".
- 1x06: Dan arriving with the pig on the first night (the book: a peck of potatoes the first night, the shote the second); Jube "well-dressed" free man's son, Jube's coat, and Jube's staff.
- 1x07: Mars Dugal' handing Hannibal a note and Old Miss handing hers to "a boy" (the book has Old Miss send the note by Hannibal himself); Jeff "in irons"; Julius keeping Annie's fan; "cain't" for "can't".

**Fixed in world files (about 45):** OVERVIEW (conjure turns people, not "slaves into masters"; burned house; the cost of 1x07); Aun' Peggy's casting note and bio (she is seen and speaks in the tales; the whipping remark is the narrator's); the arc entries for her practice and for Annie (Annie "arranged" nothing in the text); Colonel Pendleton's "own account"; "hoodoo" alias, never used in the book; Jeems's month away (he is back within days); Solomon's sweetheart "sold" (sent to Robeson County) and Solomon "dosing" the soup (the cook did); Tenie's conjure "fifteen years before" (it has been fifteen years or more since) and her sitting on the kitchen steps (she wandered round the schoolhouse and died there); Old Miss spotting Chloe's pining (the doctor reports it); Mabel "most willing accomplice"; Malcolm "would have been glad to hire" Julius; the free settlement "within ten miles"; the Guinea conjurer's "king's son" (king or governor) and Pete as one who "poisons"; the horse trader's quoted slur (now the book's wording); the McAdoo house and vineyard "burned"; "every frame scene" at the vineyard; the sap "ointment"; Jim Crow glossary note (the term predates the book); the task glossary's unsupported gloss; timelines (Henry's first discovery, Henry's sale prices, Jeems's return the next day, the book never names the philosopher John reads, Dugal's ten dollars is Julius's guess, Tom's reinstatement the next evening); Tom's invented physique; a new note in `notes/the-two-dans.md` on the book's own contradictions (Julius lives "over yander" yet has long occupied a cabin on the vineyard; Aun' Peggy established in 1x01 and 1x03 but a newcomer in 1x06).

**Left as the book has it:** the two Dans, the two Marrabos, the two Lucys, "Mars Jeems" in Sandy's lament; Julius's claim to have been born and raised on the McAdoo plantation while living "behine de nex' san'-hill"; "The Gray Wolf's Ha'nt" (the Gutenberg heading prints "Wolfs").

**Unverifiable:** how the foreman at the sawmill and the man cleaning the spring spoke or who they were; the book never says whether Mars Dugal' was killed or merely suspected killed by Yankees (Julius only suspects it); whether Annie and Julius conspired in 1x07 (the book suggests it, never states it). (The script edits kept every line count, so the generated `cues.json` files still match their scripts.)

## Known gaps

- The appendix material (the three uncollected Julius stories and the essay) is not adapted.
- Minor characters with no entity file: the overseers (Mr. Smif, Jackson), 'Dolphus, Aun' Lucy, Marshall, the Branson girl, Sandy Campbell.
- Cross-tale chronology is not fixed in the source; only frame events carry `chronological_order`.

## Format conversion

The Gutenberg plain-text file (`the-conjure-woman.txt`) was converted to Markdown and the `.txt` removed (the project keeps source texts as well-formatted `.md`). Paragraphs were reflowed to one line each, headings and a linked table of contents added, verse, letters and quotations kept as line-preserved block quotes, and `_italic_` markers turned into Markdown emphasis. The Project Gutenberg header, footer and full license are kept verbatim in the "License and provenance" section at the end of the `.md`. The Library of America advertisement at the end of the Gutenberg file is kept as a fenced text block (its italics markers span several paragraphs). The story heading "The Gray Wolfs Ha'nt" reproduces the Gutenberg body heading as printed there (the contents list reads "Wolf's").

**Verification.** A script compared the word sequence of the `.md` body with the original text between the Gutenberg START/END markers (whitespace and markup characters normalized; added heading text excluded; headings compared case-insensitively). Result: **55,282 words in the body, identical in order and spelling**; 74 words of title page and the two Gutenberg contents lists were replaced by the generated contents list, and the 11-word "End of Project Gutenberg's…" line moved to the license section. The license section was compared character for character with the original header and footer (identical). Nothing was corrected, modernized or respelled.
