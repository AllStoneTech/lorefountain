Reuel's closest friend at Harvard and, on the surface, his benefactor — it's Aubrey who arranges Reuel's paid post on the [[Meroe]] expedition, and Aubrey who has quietly known Reuel's ancestry, without ever saying so, since they first met. What the novel reveals only through [[Hannah]] is that he is himself [[Mira]]'s son, swapped at birth for the Livingstons' own infant, who died within the hour. Engaged to [[Molly Vance]] while obsessed with [[Dianthe Lusk]], he kicks free of Molly's grasp in a canoe accident and lets her sink, saves and hides Dianthe, and — once Reuel is safely in Africa — pays [[Jim Titus]] to intercept his mail and, if he can manage it, put him out of the way. He tells Dianthe Reuel is dead and marries her by fraud. When Dianthe is found dead by poison, Telassar's ancient law does what no American court will: compelled by [[Ai]]'s hypnotic command of "death by thine own hand," he drowns himself in the same stretch of the [[Charles River]] that took Molly.
