## Premise

[[Reuel Briggs]], a brilliant, friendless Harvard medical student, conceals two things: a private gift for mesmerism, and an ancestry he has always half-suspected and hidden deliberately. Both concealments — see [[The Hidden Self]] and [[Racial Passing]] — drive a story that takes him from a Boston hospital ward to the hidden African city of [[Telassar]], and reveals that three people who don't know they're related share the same buried royal bloodline.

## Setting

Turn-of-the-century Boston high society and the Vance family estate outside it; the [[Charles River]], where two accidents (one staged) change everything; and, in the second half of the story, an expedition to [[Meroe]] that finds [[Telassar]] — a hidden, technologically and culturally advanced African city untouched by the outside world, ruled in absence by [[Queen Candace]] and prophesied to await the return of an heir of the ancient king Ergamenes.

## Key Characters

- **[[Reuel Briggs]]** — a Harvard medical student passing as white; a mesmerist; ultimately revealed as the last surviving heir of the [[House of Telassar]] and crowned King Ergamenes.
- **[[Dianthe Lusk]]** — a jubilee soloist Reuel revives after a train wreck leaves her presumed dead and memoryless; secretly marries Reuel, is later coerced into marrying Aubrey, and dies forcing the truth of their shared blood into the open.
- **[[Aubrey Livingston]]** — Reuel's closest friend and secret rival, engaged to Molly Vance while obsessed with Dianthe; unknowingly Reuel and Dianthe's own half-brother, and the story's antagonist.
- **[[Hannah]]** — grandmother to all three, and the one person who knows and eventually reveals the truth of their shared parentage.

## Synopsis

Reuel Briggs revives Dianthe Lusk after a train wreck leaves her presumed dead, falls in love with her, and marries her in secret before leaving for an expedition to Meroe funded by his friend Aubrey Livingston — who has quietly known Reuel's ancestry for years without ever saying so. In Reuel's absence, a canoe accident drowns Aubrey's fiancée Molly Vance and leaves Dianthe presumed dead as well, though Aubrey has actually pulled her from the water and hidden her away; he tells her Reuel is dead and coerces her into marrying him. In Africa, grief and a lotus-lily birthmark he's carried since childhood lead Reuel into Telassar, where he is crowned King Ergamenes — heir to a royal line whose last unknowing daughter was his own mother, Mira. He returns to America too late: intercepted mail exposes Aubrey's fraud, and Hannah reveals that Reuel, Dianthe, and Aubrey are all Mira's children. Dianthe tries to poison Aubrey herself; he forces her to drink it instead, and she dies the night Reuel finally makes it home. Ancient Telassar law, invoked through Ai's hypnotic sentence, compels Aubrey to drown himself in the same stretch of the Charles River that took Molly. Reuel returns to Telassar to rule beside Queen Candace, uneasy to the end about how much of the outside world is closing in on his people's hiding place.

## Themes

The novel's title and central argument, spoken by Reuel himself in its opening chapter, is that "the hidden self" lies quiescent in everyone — see [[The Hidden Self]]. Reuel hides his ancestry and his mesmeric gift; Dianthe loses access to her own identity entirely; Aubrey hides his crimes behind a friend's face and never learns that his own hidden self — Mira's blood, swapped into the Livingston line at birth — is the deepest concealment in the book. [[Racial Passing]] runs through all three as the same wound in different forms: chosen and costly for Reuel, unknowing for Dianthe once her memory is gone, and entirely unsuspected for Aubrey, who believes himself white by birth. [[Ethiopian Primacy]] gives the hidden city of Telassar its historical weight — not a fantasy, but, in the novel's own argument, the last living proof of a real, buried African history.
