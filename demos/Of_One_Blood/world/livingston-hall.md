Where [[Aubrey Livingston]] brings [[Dianthe Lusk]] after their wedding, explicitly called his "ancestral home in Maryland" (Chapter XX; "Livingston Place" in Chapter XXI) — which sits uneasily against Chapter VII, where Aubrey will "remove to Virginia and renew the ancient splendor of his ancestral home," and the "Laurel Hill, Virginia" address of Chapters VIII and XI. Marked `canon_status: contradicted` rather than silently resolved in one direction; both are modeled as the novel presents them. This is where Dianthe finds Jim Titus's letter, meets Aunt Hannah, and dies, and where Aunt Hannah's cabin lies within walking distance.
