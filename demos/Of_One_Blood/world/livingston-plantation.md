The elder Dr. Livingston's "large plantation of slaves," where [[Mira]] and her mother [[Hannah]] were enslaved and Mira's children were born ("de Livingston place," as Hannah calls it). The novel never names it or places it: Aubrey says only that his father "owned a large plantation." The name Laurel Hill, Virginia, belongs to Aubrey's ancestral home (the address on his telegram to [[Jim Titus]] and on his letter), where he says in Chapter VII he'll "renew the ancient splendor" after marrying — so "Laurel Hill" is kept here as an alias with that caveat. Chapter XXI, though, has Hannah living within walking distance of [[Livingston Hall]], the "ancestral home" the story later places in Maryland; the text never reconciles the two.
