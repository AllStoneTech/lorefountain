Living as white while being of Black ancestry — the condition [[Reuel Briggs]] chooses deliberately, at real professional cost, to survive Harvard and Boston society; the condition [[Dianthe Lusk]] falls into unknowingly once her memory is gone; and, the novel's sharpest twist, the condition [[Aubrey Livingston]] lives in without ever suspecting it, since he was raised from infancy as the Livingstons' own son. The novel treats passing not as an ending point but as a wound its revelations — the sibling reveal, the coronation, Aubrey's death sentence under a law of the very bloodline he never knew was his — exist to force back open.
